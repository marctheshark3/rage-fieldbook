# A long brief still needs a retrieval check

*Reading note · Published paper inspected · Experiments not independently reproduced.*

**Source:** Nelson F. Liu, Kevin Lin, John Hewitt, Ashwin Paranjape, Michele Bevilacqua, Fabio Petroni, and Percy Liang, [Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9/), *TACL* 12 (2024), 157–173. DOI: `10.1162/tacl_a_00638`. The [source record](../../sources/README.md#lost-in-the-middle) identifies the inspected PDF and rights.

## What the paper examines

The authors vary input length and the location of relevant material in multi-document question answering and synthetic key-value retrieval. Several tested models perform better when useful information appears near a context's edges than in its middle; behavior differs by model and task. These are historical experiments, not a benchmark of a reader's current tools. Sections 2 and 3 describe the tasks and results. [Primary paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.9.pdf).

## The question it raises here

Our editorial inference is to check whether a supplied decision can actually be retrieved before relying on a long continuation. This paper does not establish the cause of Marc's gear result or the value of personal taste records. Those require different evidence.

## Try a small check in your own work

Choose one decision the next task must preserve, such as the accepted inclusion rule for a digest. Give it a short identity and put it in the current context brief beside its reason. Keep its wording separate from abandoned alternatives.

Before asking for an extended continuation, request the current decision, the record supporting it, and any conflict with the new brief. Inspect both the returned reference and subsequent artifact. Finding a sentence and applying it correctly are separate things to examine.

If the agent cannot identify the record, check whether the source was available, which revision it received, and whether the current choice was distinguishable. A confident answer without a supporting record leaves those questions unresolved.

This is a proposed working practice. No fresh-session retrieval trial was run for this note. The [proposed context-position protocol](../proposals/context-position.md) keeps all conditions supplied with the same records. Its eventual result would answer a narrower question than whether accumulated context improves an entire project.

Keep [context](../../chapters/05-context-and-memory.md) useful by preserving current decisions and their sources. Treat this note as a reason to inspect a dependency in your workflow, with outcomes to record in your own setting.
