# Context brief

Use this alongside a [work brief](work-brief.md) when taste, prior decisions, or a continuing project should inform the next task. Keep it short and link to selected material. The headings below are a template, not a record of Marc's private preferences.

- **Purpose and date:** Which work does this context support, and when was it last checked?
- **Current direction:** What is the person trying to achieve now?
- **Accepted references:** Two or three relevant artifacts, with a sentence about why each fits.
- **Directions to avoid:** A useful counterexample and the reason it misses.
- **Preferences and their scope:** Which choices are personal, project-specific, or only relevant to this task?
- **Decisions to preserve:** What was already chosen, and where is its authoritative record?
- **Current constraints:** What limits the next attempt? Which earlier constraints have changed?
- **Procedure:** Which existing skill or documented workflow should apply?
- **Available material:** What can this agent actually read in this session?
- **Sharing boundary:** Which references may be used privately, and which outputs may be published?
- **Uncertainty:** Which preference or assumption should the agent check rather than infer?

## Keep one decision current

Use this small record when an earlier choice could conflict with the next task. The [notebook example](../chapters/05-context-and-memory.md#build-a-small-notebook-with-a-current-decision) shows it beside a source, observation, and open question.

```text
Decision ID and date:
Status: current / superseded by [ID]
Scope:
Chosen direction and reason:
Supporting source or observation, with location and identity:
Replaces: [earlier decision ID, if any]
Revisit when:
```

Point the active brief to the current record. Mark the previous record as superseded and retain its reason as history. Record a new observation when the artifact changes; an accepted decision alone does not establish that anyone implemented it.

## After the attempt

Record what was accepted, the reason it worked, and a correction worth carrying forward. Update a preference where it belongs; update a procedure when its steps were the problem. Retire superseded guidance visibly. Keep incidental conversation out of the brief unless it changes a future decision.
